# AI Project Context — Popeye Vintage Jazz

Use this document as the creative north star for the entire project. It explains what the channel is, what it should feel like, and how music, imagery, storytelling, and YouTube metadata must stay consistent.

## 1. Channel identity

This is a **Vintage Jazz** music channel set inside the quiet, weathered maritime world of **Popeye1**, an old sailor with many memories.

- Primary genre and brand keyword: **Vintage Jazz**.
- Default branch: **Late Night Jazz**.
- Conditional branch: **Jazz Noir**, used only when the music and episode are genuinely darker and more mysterious.
- Main character: Popeye1 — old, calm, weathered, restrained, and reflective.
- Permanent world cue: maritime life, present directly or indirectly in every episode.
- Emotional core: mature nostalgia, quiet solitude, warmth, slight bittersweetness, and comfort.

The channel should feel like an old memory returning slowly after midnight. It is never rushed, flashy, heroic, tragic, or melodramatic.

## 2. Core emotional promise

The viewer should feel that they have entered a private, peaceful moment in the life of an old sailor:

- a nearly empty waterfront room after midnight;
- distant harbor lights through mist or rain;
- a quiet fireplace, ship cabin, pier, seawall, or closing tavern;
- old objects and unfinished memories;
- nowhere to hurry and nothing to prove.

The mood may be lonely, but it must remain safe and comforting. Sadness should be restrained and mature, never hopeless or emotionally extreme.

Three short principles summarize the channel:

> Music first. Scene second. Ambience third.

> Begin low. Stay low. End low.

> No hurry. No drama. No destination.

## 3. Musical direction

All music is strictly instrumental Vintage Jazz.

### Permanent musical DNA

- Slow tempo, normally **50–54 BPM**.
- Low-to-middle register from beginning to end.
- Flat, restrained energy curve with no late build-up.
- Simple memorable melodies, small intervals, spacious phrasing, and generous pauses.
- Restrained improvisation and small ensembles.
- Warm analog character, rich low-mids, and smooth soft highs.
- Slightly weathered, intimate, and natural recording character.
- Warm major 7th, minor 7th, major 9th, 6/9, and gentle dominant colors for the default late-night branch.
- Ambience remains distant and secondary to musical quality.

Useful ensemble families include acoustic trio, Rhodes trio, vibraphone, bass-forward, and intentionally sax-led arrangements. Saxophone is never added automatically. Instruments should sound like musicians playing jazz, not imitating waves or scenery.

### Absolutely no vocals

No singing, spoken word, whispering, humming, chanting, choir, vocalise, scat, ad-libs, narration, or vocal samples.

### Energy restrictions

Avoid crescendos, climaxes, soaring melodies, high-register peaks, aggressive solos, virtuosic runs, punchy drums, busy arrangements, and endings that become louder, brighter, or more emotional than the opening.

### Branches

**Late Night Jazz** is the default: warm, mellow, intimate, reflective, nostalgic, slightly bittersweet, and comforting. Do not inject detective, danger, suspense, or heavy noir language into this branch.

**Jazz Noir** is conditional: smoky, shadowed, mysterious, low-register, and mildly tense, but still slow and restrained. Noir must never become aggressive, frightening, tragic, cinematic, or climax-driven.

### Historical consistency

- A 1940s or 1950s claim requires period-compatible acoustic instrumentation.
- Fender Rhodes belongs to a late-1960s/1970s setting or to a non-calendar time anchor such as “after midnight.”
- Never make a precise historical claim that conflicts with the instruments.

## 4. Visual identity

Every final thumbnail is a 16:9 vintage-inspired 2D illustration featuring the recognizable Popeye1 sailor.

### Popeye1 character

Preserve these identity anchors:

- large rounded chin;
- prominent curved nose;
- one naturally squinted eye;
- small ears;
- oversized muscular forearms;
- compact sailor proportions;
- white sailor cap with black band;
- small pipe.

He is a weathered, calm old sailor — not a superhero, gangster, pirate, comedian, or muscle-flexing action figure. His expression is restrained and reflective. Prefer a frontal face or gentle three-quarter angle because the canonical reference supports those views best.

Wardrobe may change with the episode while remaining practical, old, and maritime: midnight-navy peacoat, cream knit or sailor layer, dark slate/teal trousers, tobacco-brown shoes, deck jacket, heavy knit, sailor raincoat, or work shirt. Avoid bright primary colors, tuxedos, luxury fashion, pirate clothing, and modern streetwear.

### Illustration language

- Mid-century-inspired hand-drawn 2D illustration with modern composition clarity.
- Strong but slightly imperfect hand-inked contours.
- Classic cel-style character rendering with broad matte color areas.
- Gouache or opaque-watercolor environments.
- Restrained dry-brush shadows, subtle paper grain, sparse halftone, and slight registration drift.
- Clean, well-preserved vintage print feeling — not a damaged antique document.

### Palette

Core colors:

- deep tobacco brown;
- dark umber;
- charcoal and faded black;
- muted navy, midnight blue, indigo, and weathered teal;
- faded blue-gray and aged cream.

Accent colors are limited to muted amber, burnt orange, faded ochre, and dull brass. Outdoor/coastal scenes should remain approximately 75–85% cool muted blue/charcoal tones. Warm accents guide the eye; they do not flood the image.

Use one weak practical light source to reveal only the face, cap edge, one forearm, or the primary story prop. Preserve large painted shadow masses. Think **painted light**, not simulated cinematic light.

### Maritime identity

Every episode needs a quiet maritime cue, such as dark water, fog, a harbor window, distant ship lights, dock timber, ropes, charts, a lighthouse trace, rain near the waterfront, or the sailor’s connection to life at sea. The ocean does not need to dominate the image.

### Composition

- Popeye1 is the emotional anchor and must remain readable on mobile.
- Use one clear micro-story and only a few believable props.
- Keep a clean silhouette, focal path, and useful negative space.
- Props must logically belong to the location and action.
- A whiskey glass belongs on a believable surface in a bar, room, lounge, or cabin — never loose on a pier, beach, wet street, or exposed deck.
- Do not repeat the same bar/window/whiskey pose as a fixed channel template.

### Visual exclusions

Never use 3D, CGI, photorealism, anime, Pixar/modern Disney style, modern vector gradients, glossy plastic surfaces, HDR, bloom, volumetric lighting, neon, modern orange-and-teal grading, generic digital painting, luxury interiors, oversized instruments, performing bands, party energy, pirate imagery, heroic action, dramatic storms, giant moons, or spectacular seascapes.

Do not use torn paper, cracked edges, scratches, scuffs, dirt, yellowed archive damage, pervasive grunge, horror decay, or fake “old photo” filters. Vintage describes the illustration and print language, not physical destruction.

## 5. Episode storytelling

Each video is a distinct episode inside the same universe. An episode profile should define:

- mood branch;
- emotional premise;
- time anchor;
- maritime anchor;
- location and weather;
- a small unfinished story;
- Popeye1’s situation, pose, and action;
- one or two logical props;
- visual direction;
- dominant musical family and supporting musical evidence.

Good stories are quiet and incomplete: waiting for the last boat, reading an old letter, watching rain on harbor glass, resting after a night shift, remembering someone absent, or sitting near the tide before dawn.

Changing only one prop or synonym does not create a genuinely new episode. Vary location, action, camera, relationship cue, weather, wardrobe, time anchor, and narrative premise while preserving the channel identity.

## 6. Source of truth and production logic

The project follows this mandatory direction:

```text
available real tracks
→ locked playlist
→ duration-weighted playlist profile
→ episode profile
→ thumbnail
→ YouTube title and description
→ render and upload
```

Never invent a visual story first and force unrelated tracks to fit it. The locked playlist determines the episode’s emotional and sonic identity. Thumbnail and SEO must describe the same episode and may not add unsupported instruments, weather, ambience, era, or noir claims.

If the music library cannot support a coherent episode, report the missing music family, mood, instrumentation, or duration instead of fabricating content.

## 7. YouTube positioning

The default metadata language is English and targets a worldwide audience.

### Keyword hierarchy

- Primary: `vintage jazz`.
- Recurring secondary: `late night jazz`.
- Conditional secondary: `jazz noir` only for a real noir episode.
- Instrument, rain, cafe, lounge, sleep, study, or relaxation keywords are used only when the actual content supports them.

Do not redefine the channel as smooth jazz, lo-fi, dark jazz, cafe jazz, or saxophone jazz merely because a keyword has higher search volume.

### Title style

Default structure:

```text
vintage jazz — [emotional or immersive world-building sentence]
```

- Entire title is lowercase.
- `vintage jazz` normally appears first.
- Include a natural time anchor and direct or indirect maritime anchor.
- Aim for approximately 60–90 characters and stay below 100.
- Use an immersive scene, memory, emotional line, or unfinished story.
- Avoid clickbait, emojis, ALL CAPS, title case, keyword stuffing, and unsupported promises.

Example tone:

```text
vintage jazz — the last ship arrived shortly after midnight
```

### Description style

Start naturally with “Vintage jazz,” then use only 1–3 concise sentences describing the verified scene, emotional tone, and appropriate listening context. Do not write a keyword list or claim instruments and ambience that the playlist does not establish.

## 8. What this channel is not

This is not:

- generic lo-fi background music;
- upbeat swing or party jazz;
- flashy virtuoso jazz;
- pirate or nautical adventure content;
- tragic cinematic noir;
- photorealistic maritime art;
- a repeated whiskey-bar thumbnail template;
- a channel built around saxophone, rain, sleep, study, or cafe keywords;
- random music placed under an unrelated Popeye image.

## 9. Instructions for any AI working on this project

Before producing an asset or decision:

1. Preserve Vintage Jazz as the permanent genre.
2. Default to Late Night Jazz; use Noir only with explicit evidence.
3. Keep the emotion mature, quiet, slightly bittersweet, and comforting.
4. Keep music slow, low-register, restrained, and completely instrumental.
5. Keep Popeye1 recognizable and emotionally grounded.
6. Include a subtle maritime identity in every episode.
7. Keep the visual language hand-drawn, matte, mid-century-inspired, and clean.
8. Make props, era, instruments, weather, and story logically consistent.
9. Let the real playlist determine the episode — never the reverse.
10. Keep music, episode profile, thumbnail, title, and description aligned as one world.

When uncertain, choose restraint over spectacle, warmth over darkness, implication over explanation, and one meaningful detail over many decorative props.
